## The language-model build path

Eight stages, read in order, top to bottom. Each one consumes the previous
stage's output, so the reading order and the build order are the same thing.

Every chapter states what it costs before you start it, and the minutes below
are computed from the prose rather than estimated. Every chapter also carries a
level — **Foundation** for a mechanism everything downstream assumes,
**Applied** for a decision with a measured tradeoff, **Frontier** for a claim at
the edge of what a run here can establish — so you can tell a prerequisite from
an open question at a glance.

Some stages hang two kinds of side-trip beneath them, and neither is a step you
double back for. A **detour** leaves the mission for a mechanism you need
before the next decision, and names what you bring back. A **companion** chapter
continues the same stage's argument once the mechanism is in hand. Skip both on
a first pass and the spine still holds. Almost every chapter here is backed by
a run, so the sidebar marks the exception: a chapter labelled `draft` has
working code and no recorded run behind its claim yet.

## Know what the evidence proves

**Every capability claim is backed by a run. Every mission is backed by a
measurable outcome.** A verified label means the exact command, hardware,
wall-clock, cost, and metrics are recorded. A draft may contain working code,
but its declared path is not yet evidenced.

Business outcomes cannot be executed on a GPU. Missions therefore declare a
reproducible proxy, guardrails, and what the result does *not* establish before
the build begins.
