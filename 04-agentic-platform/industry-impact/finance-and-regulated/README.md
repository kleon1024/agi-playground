# The audit infrastructure is the unlock

> Dated survey, 2026-08-14. Sources cited inline.

**Question:** banks and insurers are adopting agents *because* they are
regulated, not despite it. Why does audit readiness make regulated sectors
the leaders, and what do their deployments look like?

## Why regulation flips to an advantage

Regulated sectors already operate under oversight requirements that align
with what trustworthy agents need: traceability, audit trails, and
human-signoff gates. The EU AI Act and sector rules moved guardrails from
optional to required ([governance trends](https://bigid.com/blog/agentic-ai-governance-trends/)),
and the infrastructure that satisfies them is the same infrastructure a
governed agent needs.

## The deployments

Ramp's finance agent targets audit hours and compliance scoring;
Bank of America's Erica is used by over 90% of employees with a \$4B AI
investment ([use-case roundup](https://www.guvi.in/blog/ai-agents-unleashed/)).
Agent registries and lineage tools (e.g., Decube) give banks an auditable
record of every agent touching their data — for a regional bank under MAS
oversight, that is regulatory exposure, not abstract risk
([Disrupts](https://disrupts.disruptsmedia.com/datatech/decube-targets-ai-agent-sprawl-governance-layer-regulated-firms)).

## What this means for this topic

The control-plane stage's invariants (no suppressed audit, no self-granted
authority) are the regulated-industry requirements made architectural. The
adoption matrix's audit-readiness variable is why this cell is green.

## What this does not say

It does not claim regulation makes adoption easy — it raises the bar and
the cost. It maps why the bar is aligned with what governed agents need.
