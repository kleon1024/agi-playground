## Two readings

**The third verdict names exactly what would turn it into the other two.**
The 18 missing inputs are not a wall of excuses; they are the acceptance
contract enumerated — per-fold baselines, deflated Sharpe and its trial
count, drawdown, capacity, point-in-time integrity, cost, latency, regimes.
Each named input is a different way the mission could fail, and the refusal
lists them so a reader can see the gap, not guess at it.

**A verdict drawn from missing inputs is wrong, not cautious.** The report
refuses to render MET or NOT MET because manufacturing a conclusion from
partial artifacts would hide the uncertainty the mission exists to expose.
`CANNOT DETERMINE` is the honest output for this state — the same
three-way discipline mission 02's report uses, here with financial
multiple-testing on top.

## Check your mental model

Answer each before opening it.

**1. Why is CANNOT DETERMINE better than a confident guess?**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Because a guess with a verdict looks like evidence and isn't. The report's
18 named missing inputs are the mission's acceptance conditions that have
not been met; a confident MET or NOT MET over them would be a wrong number
wearing a decisive label. The refusal is what keeps the reader from
believing a conclusion the data cannot support.

</details>

**2. What does the trial count have to do with the verdict?**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It is the input the deflated Sharpe needs — the number of variants searched
before the winner was chosen. Without it, any Sharpe would be overstated by
selection bias. The report names it among the missing inputs precisely
because a verdict without it is a multiple-testing failure waiting to be
reported as a result.

</details>
