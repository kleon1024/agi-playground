## Purpose

The stage audit swept sample sizes empirically. This detour computes the
confidence-interval width for the same 0.4-point increment (0.032
exposed versus 0.028 control) at four sample sizes, and the per-arm
sample size a proper experiment needs for 80 and 90 percent power.

## Output

```
lift too small to see: the CI, computed analytically
  true rates: exposed 0.032, control 0.028
  true increment: 0.004 (0.4 points)

CI width for the 0.4-point increment (fixed seed):
  n per arm | CI half-width | CI            | covers zero
       1000 | +0.0142      | -0.0082 to +0.0202 | yes
      10000 | +0.0047      | -0.0055 to +0.0039 | yes
     100000 | +0.0015      | +0.0026 to +0.0055 | NO
    1000000 | +0.0005      | +0.0035 to +0.0044 | NO

sample size to detect the 0.4-point increment:
  80% power: 28,547 users per arm
  90% power: 38,216 users per arm

CI half-width vs effect size at n = 10,000 per arm:
  increment | half-width | the CI can see it?
  0.004    | 0.0047     | NO
  0.010    | 0.0049     | yes
  0.020    | 0.0053     | yes
  0.050    | 0.0062     | yes

reading: the stage's own 0.4-point increment needs about
28,000 users per arm (80% power) to be seen at all, and the
CI at 10,000 users is wider than the entire effect. A 1-point
increment is visible at the same scale: the experiment is
sized for the effect, so small-lift campaigns need either a
much larger holdout or a cheaper metric.
```

## Notes

- CI half-width shrinks from 1.42 points at 1,000 users per arm to
  0.05 points at a million. The 0.4-point increment only excludes zero
  once the sample reaches roughly 100,000 users per arm.
- 80 percent power needs 28,547 users per arm; 90 percent needs 38,216.
  This is the analytic version of the stage audit's verdict: the
  experiment is sized for the effect.
- Lewis & Rao (2015, QJE) run 25 field experiments on US\$2.8M of
  digital ad spend and find that measuring the returns to advertising
  is difficult — the CI math above is the mechanism behind that result.
