# The attribution that explains the score tells a story the score did not

**Question:** [stage 52's attribution](../) broke the score into
contributions. This chapter asks how explanations go wrong, and answers:
when the explanation is generated by the largest coefficient instead of
the largest contribution, it is a rationalization — and a user who checks
it catches the difference.

**Before this:** [stage 52 — trust and explainability](../) and its
executed contribution read.

## The two answers, executed

The run ([record](runs/2026-08-07-explanation-is-wrong-read.md)) computes
coefficients and contributions for one item:

| feature | value | weight | contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| price | 2.0 | -0.040 | -0.0800 |
| category affinity | 0.9 | +0.010 | +0.0090 |
| similar users bought | 0.3 | +0.200 | +0.0600 |

Largest coefficient: similar users bought (weight +0.200). Largest
contribution: price (value x weight, -0.0800).

## The reading

The explanation says "similar users bought" because its coefficient is
largest, but the price term moved the score further. The user checks the
claim against what they see — the price — and the explanation fails.
Attribution must explain the contribution, not the coefficient: a weight
is how strongly the model can use a feature; the contribution is how much
it actually did for this item. Confusing the two ships a headline that
the score never acted on.

## The fix and its trade

The fix is to explain the contribution, not the coefficient, and to
validate the explanation against the served decision: does the story
match what the score actually did for this item. The executed read
prices the failure — "similar users bought" holds the largest weight
(+0.200) but a small value (0.3), so its contribution is 0.0600, while
price's contribution is -0.0800 and is the largest by magnitude. The
coefficient headline ships a claim the score never acted on, and the
user's local evidence — the price in front of them — catches it.

The trade is that contribution-based explanation costs per-item
computation and a validation pass the coefficient shortcut skips: the
weight is how strongly the model can use a feature, the contribution is
how much it actually did here, and only the latter is checkable by the
user. The explanation system is validated against the served decision
rather than against the model's parameters, and the validation is what
keeps the cheap-to-generate headline from becoming a lie the user can
check.

## Who owns the loop

- **The explainability and ML team** owns the contribution computation
  and the validation of the headline against the served decision.
- **The product and UX team** owns what the user can check locally,
  since a claim contradicting the visible price is the failure shape.
- **The measurement team** owns the explanation-vs-decision audit that
  catches the coefficient shortcut before it ships.

## Evidence boundary

The executed comparison over one declared item (illustrative,
deterministic). It demonstrates the mechanism; real explanation systems
must be validated against the served decision — does the explanation's
story match the score's actual cause — rather than against the model's
parameters.

## Check your mental model

Answer each before opening it.

**1. How can the largest coefficient and the largest contribution
differ?**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Because the contribution is value times weight, and the value varies per
item. "Similar users bought" has the biggest weight (+0.200) but a small
value (0.3), so its contribution is 0.0600; price has a small weight but
a big value, so its contribution is -0.0800 — larger in magnitude. The
weight is the model's capability; the contribution is what happened here.

</details>

**2. Why does the wrong headline fail specifically with a user?**

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Because the user has local evidence: they can see the price and their own
history, and the claim "similar users bought" is uncheckable while the
price is right there. An explanation that contradicts what the user can
observe reads as the system not knowing why it showed the item — which
is the trust failure the trust-erodes detour measures in opt-outs.

</details>
