## Purpose

Derive the 39,244-users-per-arm figure the split-lies detour asserts, then
sweep the four design levers that change it — minimum detectable effect,
metric variance, CUPED variance reduction, and traffic allocation plus
controlled rollout — and translate each into calendar days on a fixed
throughput of 10,000 eligible users per day.

## Notes

- The 39,244 figure is n = 2(z_alpha/2 + z_beta)^2 / delta^2 with the same
  z constants the split-lies detour hardcodes; the two chapters now agree
  to the user.
- The "2%" in "a 2% lift" is a standardized effect: 2% of the outcome's
  standard deviation, which is 0.39 percentage points at a 4% CTR and 0.60
  points at a 10% baseline. Sample size lives in variance units, which is
  why the metric-variance lever matters.
- CUPED factor (1 - rho^2) and its sample-size saving come from Deng, Xu,
  Kohavi and Walker (2013), "Improving the Sensitivity of Online Controlled
  Experiments by Utilizing Pre-Experiment Data", WSDM 2013.
- Controlled-rollout time cost follows Xia, Bhardwaj, Dmitriev and Fabijan
  (2019), "Safe Velocity: A Practical Guide to Software Deployment at Scale
  using Controlled Rollout", ICSE-SEIP 2019, pp. 11-20; the ramp model here
  is a linear 0-to-100% ramp and a step 10%-hold, both deterministic.
- Sample-size and design practice, including why under-sizing produces
  underpowered tests, follow Zhou, Lu and Shallah (2023), "All about
  sample-size calculations for A/B testing: Novel extensions and practical
  guide", arXiv:2305.16459, accepted at CIKM 2023.
- Synthetic and deterministic: the numbers price the design tradeoffs of
  one fixed scenario, not a real experiment's outcome.
