## Purpose

The two-stage funnel exists because fine_rank costs ~12x per candidate what
pre_rank does (14us vs 1.2us in the stage's own specs). The pre-rank cut is
the dial that decides whether the funnel fits its latency budget; this run
sweeps it and reads the p95.

## Notes

- The cut-300 row reproduces the recorded run's p95 (49.31ms) exactly, which
  is the determinism check that this sweep runs the same model.
- The cut's cost lands on fine-rank: its p95 grows 4.1 to 24.4ms across the
  sweep, while the other stages are cut-independent.
- Tightening below ~100 buys little (p95 45.8 at 50 vs 46.4 at 100): recall,
  value-tree, and mixing fixed costs dominate once fine-rank is small. The
  latency-vs-cut curve flattens at the low end, which is where recall
  quality argues for a bigger cut without paying much latency.
- The model is illustrative (synthetic timings); the p95-300ms mission
  budget sits far above these numbers. The durable finding is the shape:
  the cut is the funnel's latency dial, and its marginal cost is flat at
  the low end.
