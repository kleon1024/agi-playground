# Run — the winner's-log CVR bias audit

**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Command:** `uv run python core/cvr_bias.py`
**Hardware:** Apple M1 Pro, macOS 15.6.1, CPU-only.
**Software:** Python 3.12.9 via uv; stdlib only.
**Wall-clock:** 0.13s.
**Cost:** \$0 (local lane).

## Purpose

The stage run derives the bid as value times conversion rate. The audit
asks the case-finding question at production scale: what CVR does the
bidder actually see? A bidder only logs the auctions it won, and it
wins the auctions where its bid — hence its conversion estimate — was
high. If the estimate tracks true propensity, the won set is skewed to
high-propensity impressions and the naive CVR from the winner's log
overstates the rate the campaign will actually experience. Simulation:
100,000 auctions (fixed seed); 90 percent of impressions convert at
0.012, 10 percent at 0.08; the bidder bids value (\$5) times its
estimate and wins where the price is below the bid.

## Output

```
winner's-log audit: 100,000 auctions, fixed seed
90% of impressions convert at 0.012, 10% at 0.08;
the bidder wins where its bid — its estimate — was high

                CVR read      CVR  bid ($5 x CVR)
     true (all auctions)   0.0188            0.09
    naive (won auctions)   0.0316            0.16
           IPW-corrected   0.0187            0.09

wins logged: 35672 of 100000
overbid from naive CVR: 1.68x

reading: the winner's log is a biased sample. The bidder
wins where its estimate was high, so the won set converts
better than the market — naive CVR overstates the campaign's
real rate, and the target-CPA bid overpays every auction it
actually wins. The fix is a selection correction (inverse
propensity by win rate) or a CVR model fit on the full
impression space, not just wins.
```

## Notes

- The true CVR over all auctions is 0.0188 (bid \$0.09), but the naive
  read from the 35,672 won auctions is 0.0316 (bid \$0.16) — a 1.68x
  overbid. The bidder wins where its estimate was high, and the high
  estimate marks high-propensity impressions.
- The inverse-propensity correction, weighting each won observation by
  the inverse of its win probability, recovers 0.0187 — the true rate,
  and the bid with it. The fix is a selection correction or a CVR model
  fit on the full impression space, not just wins.
- Propensities, prices, and conversions drawn with a fixed seed.
  Illustrative and deterministic, not real bid logs.
